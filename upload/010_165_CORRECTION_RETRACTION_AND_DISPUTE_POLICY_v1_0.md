# 5. WHAT COUNTS AS A CORRECTION

Correction dapat berupa:

### TYPE A — TYPOGRAPHICAL

Contoh:

- typo,
- punctuation,
- formatting.

### TYPE B — METADATA

Contoh:

- salah tahun,
- salah author spelling,
- salah repository,
- broken source metadata.

### TYPE C — FACTUAL

Contoh:

- tanggal salah,
- lokasi salah,
- identitas salah,
- hubungan guru-murid salah,
- bibliographic fact salah.

### TYPE D — ATTRIBUTION

Contoh:

- statement attributed to wrong person,
- source assigned incorrectly.

### TYPE E — INTERPRETIVE

Interpretation membutuhkan perubahan karena evidence baru atau argument research berubah.

### TYPE F — RIGHTS / PRIVACY

Publikasi harus dibatasi, diubah, atau diturunkan karena rights/privacy issue yang valid.

---

# 6. WHAT COUNTS AS A CLARIFICATION

Clarification dipakai ketika:

- fakta dasarnya tidak berubah,
- tetapi wording dapat menyebabkan salah tafsir,
- konteks tidak cukup,
- attribution perlu dipertegas,
- uncertainty perlu diperjelas.

Contoh:

Original:

> “Tradisi X menyatakan...”

Clarification:

> “Riwayat tersebut terdapat dalam tradisi yang terdokumentasi di sumber X; statusnya sebagai historical claim belum dapat dipastikan secara independen.”

---

# 7. WHAT COUNTS AS A SUBSTANTIAL REVISION

Gunakan substantial revision apabila:

- evidence baru mengubah bagian penting,
- chronology berubah,
- attribution berubah,
- article framing berubah,
- major source ditambahkan atau ditarik,
- scholarly interpretation berubah secara material.

Artikel tetap dapat dipertahankan tetapi versi baru harus diberi change note.

---

# 8. WHAT COUNTS AS WITHDRAWAL

Withdrawal adalah penghapusan publik sementara atau pembatasan akses ketika:

- verification sedang berlangsung,
- rights complaint belum selesai,
- security/privacy risk mendesak,
- content berisiko menimbulkan harm jika tetap terbuka,
- source integrity sedang diperiksa.

Withdrawal bukan otomatis berarti content salah.

---

# 9. WHAT COUNTS AS RETRACTION

Retraction digunakan bila publikasi tidak lagi dapat dipertahankan secara bertanggung jawab, misalnya karena:

- source terbukti fabricated,
- material claim unsupported secara fundamental,
- major attribution false,
- evidence materially misrepresented,
- central conclusion tidak lagi dapat dipertahankan,
- content dibuat dengan prosedur yang secara serius melanggar editorial standards.

Retraction harus terdokumentasi.

---

# 10. NOT EVERY DISAGREEMENT IS AN ERROR

Sebuah keberatan belum tentu berarti 165 salah.

Contoh:

> Source A → 1875  
> Source B → 1878

Ini dapat merupakan:

- genuine source conflict,
- calendrical issue,
- copyist error,
- institutional tradition,
- historiographical difference.

Dalam situasi ini tujuan 165 adalah:

> **document the disagreement and explain the evidence.**

# 11. WHO MAY SUBMIT A CORRECTION

Koreksi dapat diajukan oleh:

- pembaca,
- researcher,
- student,
- family custodian,
- archivist,
- institution,
- subject matter expert,
- author,
- editor,
- internal staff,
- system/audit finding.

Tidak ada requirement bahwa requester harus “orang besar”.

# 12. GOOD-FAITH STANDARD

165 menerima good-faith correction meskipun:

- requester memiliki interpretasi berbeda,
- evidence yang diberikan ternyata tidak cukup,
- koreksi akhirnya ditolak.

Good-faith process harus tetap diperlakukan secara hormat.

# 15. CORRECTION STATUS

Gunakan:

### RECEIVED
Permintaan diterima.

### TRIAGED
Kategori dan severity ditentukan.

### INVESTIGATING
Evidence sedang diperiksa.

### AWAITING INFORMATION
Data belum mencukupi.

### VERIFIED
Correction didukung evidence.

### PARTIALLY VERIFIED
Sebagian correction didukung.

### DISPUTED
Evidence tetap bertentangan.

### REJECTED
Tidak cukup dasar untuk mengubah content.

### IMPLEMENTED
Correction diterapkan.

### CLOSED
Proses selesai.

---

# 16. SEVERITY LEVEL

## S0 — Cosmetic

Tidak memengaruhi makna.

## S1 — Minor

Kesalahan kecil tetapi sebaiknya diperbaiki.

## S2 — Material

Kesalahan memengaruhi pemahaman substantif.

## S3 — Critical

Kesalahan menyentuh inti artikel, source integrity, living-person risk, legal risk, rights, privacy, atau major institutional representation.

S3 memerlukan escalation.

# 18. INITIAL RESPONSE

Setiap submission harus mendapatkan acknowledgement bila workflow memungkinkan.

Acknowledgement tidak berarti:

> 165 mengakui bahwa requester benar.

Itu hanya berarti:

> correction telah diterima untuk ditinjau.

---

# 19. EVIDENCE REQUIRED

Requester idealnya memberikan:

- source,
- citation,
- page,
- manuscript folio,
- timestamp,
- institutional record,
- archival identifier,
- photograph metadata,
- interview record,
- atau evidence relevan lainnya.

Namun keterbatasan evidence dari requester tidak otomatis menyebabkan complaint ditolak; 165 dapat melakukan independent verification.

---

# 20. INVESTIGATION STANDARD

Reviewer menilai:

- claim original,
- source original,
- evidence submitted,
- source quality,
- source independence,
- context,
- alternative evidence,
- historical chronology,
- publication history,
- rights/privacy,
- impact.

---

# 21. INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION

Untuk significant claims, 165 sebaiknya mencoba memverifikasi sendiri, bukan hanya menilai dokumen requester.

Contoh:


# 22. DECISION OPTIONS

Reviewer dapat menghasilkan:

### ACCEPT

Claim perlu diperbaiki.

### ACCEPT IN PART

Sebagian correction benar.

### CLARIFY

Fakta tidak salah secara fundamental tetapi wording perlu diperbaiki.

### RETAIN WITH DISPUTE NOTE

Evidence tetap berbeda.

### REJECT

Correction tidak cukup didukung.

### WITHDRAW PENDING REVIEW

Content dibatasi sementara.

### RETRACT

Content tidak lagi layak dipertahankan dalam bentuk publiknya.

---

# 23. ACCEPTED CORRECTION

Accepted correction harus:

1. mengubah content,
2. update last modified,
3. record correction ID,
4. update source mapping,
5. update related content bila terpengaruh,
6. update version,
7. add correction note jika material.

---

# 24. PUBLIC CORRECTION NOTE

Untuk material corrections, gunakan format sederhana:

> **Correction — [date]**  
> An earlier version stated X. Based on source Y, it has been corrected to Z.

Bahasa harus:

- netral,
- singkat,
- faktual,
- tidak defensif.

---

# 25. SILENT CORRECTION RULE

Silent correction diperbolehkan hanya untuk:

- obvious typo,
- formatting,
- non-material metadata cleanup.

Material factual correction tidak boleh disembunyikan.

---

# 26. VERSION HISTORY

Setiap major article harus mempertahankan:

```text
v1.0
v1.1
v1.2
v2.0
```

Dengan:

- date,
- summary,
- reason,
- reviewer where appropriate.

---

# 27. RETRACTION NOTICE

Retraction notice minimal mencantumkan:

- title,
- original publication date,
- retraction date,
- reason category,
- affected claims,
- replacement content if applicable,
- responsible editorial authority.

Jangan menggunakan bahasa yang mempermalukan author/requester.

---

# 28. RETRACTION VS DELETION

Retraction:

> published record existed and was withdrawn for documented reasons.

Deletion:

> physical removal of content.

Untuk integrity, prefer keeping a minimal historical record that the publication was retracted, subject to legal/privacy/safety requirements.

---

# 29. SOURCE RETIREMENT

Jika source bermasalah:

`165-SRC-#### → RETIRED`

Then:

- identify all dependent claims,
- identify dependent content,
- review citations,
- update knowledge graph,
- update search,
- record replacement if any.

---

# 30. SOURCE CORRECTION

Source metadata errors must not automatically create a second source object.

Correct the metadata while preserving:

- old value,
- new value,
- date,
- reason,
- reviewer.

---

# 31. GENEALOGY / SILSILAH DISPUTE

For lineage disputes:

1. identify exact disputed relationship,
2. collect sources,
3. distinguish institutional tradition from documentary evidence,
4. assess source provenance,
5. document conflicting versions,
6. do not manufacture certainty,
7. publish uncertainty when unresolved.

Recommended display:

```text
Version A
Source:
Evidence:
Status:

Version B
Source:
Evidence:
Status:

165 Editorial Note:
Current evidence remains inconclusive.
```

---

# 32. HISTORICAL DATE DISPUTE

Record:

- original date forms,
- calendar system,
- conversions,
- source dates,
- publication dates,
- estimated dates.

Do not normalize a disputed date into a single “fact” without explanation.

---

# 33. IDENTITY DISPUTE

For people with uncertain identity:

Do not merge records solely because:

- names are similar,
- titles are identical,
- locations overlap.

Use:

`POSSIBLE_MATCH`

until evidence is sufficient.

---

# 34. QUOTATION DISPUTE

If quotation authenticity is challenged:

1. locate original,
2. compare wording,
3. verify speaker,
4. check language,
5. check date/context,
6. check secondary transmission.

Possible outcomes:

- verified,
- attributed but source uncertain,
- paraphrase,
- misattributed,
- unverifiable,
- retracted.

---

# 35. PHOTOGRAPH IDENTIFICATION DISPUTE

Use:

- source,
- original caption,
- family/institution record,
- independent evidence,
- comparison with dated material.

If identity remains uncertain:

> “Unidentified person, possibly X”

rather than definitive identification.

---

# 36. MANUSCRIPT AUTHENTICITY DISPUTE

165 should not make definitive authenticity claims unless appropriate expertise/evidence exists.

Escalate for specialist review when necessary.

Potential status:

`AUTHENTICITY UNASSESSED`

`AUTHENTICITY UNDER REVIEW`

`AUTHENTICITY SUPPORTED`

`AUTHENTICITY DISPUTED`

---

# 37. ORAL HISTORY DISPUTE

When testimony conflicts:

Do not simply erase one testimony.

Record:

- testimony A,
- testimony B,
- dates,
- relationships to subject,
- context,
- corroboration,
- current status.

---

# 38. SCHOLARLY DISPUTE

For academic disagreement:

165 may present:

- scholar A's interpretation,
- scholar B's interpretation,
- methodological difference,
- evidence basis,
- unresolved points.

165 should not manufacture a “winner” merely for narrative simplicity.

---

# 39. INSTITUTIONAL DISPUTE

If institutions provide conflicting accounts:

- identify each institution,
- attribute each account,
- compare documentation,
- avoid defamatory language,
- escalate sensitive claims.

---

# 40. LIVING PERSON DISPUTE

Where a correction concerns a living person:

- prioritize accuracy,
- minimize unnecessary personal information,
- consider privacy,
- verify allegations carefully,
- escalate significant reputational risk.

---

# 41. LEGAL / RIGHTS COMPLAINT

Potential issues:

- copyright,
- image rights,
- privacy,
- contractual restriction,
- confidentiality,
- archive custody.

Triage separately from historical correctness.

A rights complaint does not automatically mean the historical claim is false.

---

# 42. PRIVACY REQUEST

If a valid privacy concern exists:

1. assess necessity of public disclosure,
2. assess legal requirements,
3. redact/minimize where appropriate,
4. restrict access if justified,
5. document decision.

---

# 43. SECURITY-RELATED TAKEDOWN

Immediate temporary restriction may occur when content exposes:

- passwords,
- private addresses,
- sensitive personal data,
- confidential credentials,
- exploitable technical details.

Security issue may justify urgent action before full editorial investigation.

---

# 44. GOOD-FAITH PROTECTION

165 must not retaliate against individuals who submit good-faith corrections.

Do not publish:

- private contact information,
- personal attacks,
- humiliating descriptions,

merely because someone challenged 165.

---

# 45. BAD-FAITH / ABUSIVE SUBMISSIONS

A submission may be closed without full review when it consists primarily of:

- threats,
- harassment,
- spam,
- automated abuse,
- malicious attempts to disrupt operations.

However, an aggressive tone does not by itself make the underlying correction false.

Relevant evidence should still be assessed where practical.

---

# 46. CONFLICT OF INTEREST

Reviewer must disclose relevant conflict.

A reviewer should not make the final decision on an issue involving:

- their own major publication,
- direct financial interest,
- direct family interest,
- unresolved personal dispute,

unless the governance system explicitly provides for independent review.

---

# 47. TWO-PERSON REVIEW

For S3 cases, use at least:

- one subject/domain reviewer,
- one editorial authority.

Add legal/privacy review where needed.

---

# 48. ESCALATION

Escalate based on domain:

### Historical
Research authority.

### Religious/TQN interpretation
Qualified subject reviewer.

### Archive
Archive/heritage authority.

### Rights/privacy
Legal or rights authority.

### Technology/security
Security/technology authority.

### Governance
Authority under Document 007.

# 49. APPEAL

Requester may request reconsideration when:

- new evidence exists,
- material evidence was overlooked,
- reviewer conflict is identified,
- decision misapplied policy,
- new source appears.

Appeal is a review opportunity, not a guarantee.

# 51. REVIEW DEADLINES

Internal targets should reflect severity rather than one universal deadline.

Suggested:

- S0: routine batch,
- S1: normal editorial cycle,

# 52. OUT-OF-SCOPE CLAIMS

Do not turn every disagreement into an editorial investigation.

Examples:

- theological debate unrelated to a factual statement,
- personal preference,
- criticism of writing style,
- disagreement with 165 mission,
- disagreement that provides no factual issue.

These may receive explanation without triggering correction.

---

# 53. REPUTATIONAL PROTECTION

165 must avoid:

- personal attacks,
- sensationalizing correction disputes,
- implying guilt without evidence,
- selective quoting,
- retaliatory publication.

Correction systems are for knowledge quality, not conflict escalation.

# 59. AI CORRECTION PROPAGATION

When a factual correction is accepted:

- update canonical source/claim record,
- invalidate stale retrieval cache where appropriate,
- update AI indexes,
- flag prior generated summaries,
- regenerate affected derived content where necessary.

AI should not continue presenting a known corrected claim as current.

---

# 60. AI DISPUTE ANSWERS

When AI encounters unresolved dispute:

Use:

> “Sources available to 165 differ on this point...”

Then provide:

- competing source records,
- evidence status,
- date of last review.

Do not fabricate resolution.

- specifically requested for historical/audit purpose,
- marked clearly as retracted,
- access is appropriate.

# 69. METRICS

### Correction Rate

### Repeat Error Rate

### Source Coverage

Whether material claims have evidence.

### Retraction Rate

# 70. LESSONS-LEARNED LOOP

Every significant correction should ask:

> Why did the error occur?

Possible root causes:

- inadequate source checking,
- unclear workflow,
- weak metadata,
- AI hallucination,
- translation error,
- reviewer gap,
- poor taxonomy,
- insufficient documentation.

Then improve the system.

# 72. TRAINING LOOP

Repeated correction themes should become training material.

Example:

If multiple errors arise from:

> conflating institutional tradition with documented history

then create an editorial training module.

---

# 73. CORRECTION CULTURE

Internal culture:

> **Find the error early. Fix it clearly. Learn from it permanently.**

A correction should not automatically be treated as personal failure.

Systemic improvement is the goal.

---

# 74. NO RETALIATION PRINCIPLE

165 must not:

- blacklist good-faith researchers,
- suppress legitimate corrections,
- manipulate search results to bury criticism,
- remove accurate evidence because it is inconvenient.

---

# 75. EDITORIAL ACCOUNTABILITY

When a serious error occurs, the institution should ask:

- Was policy followed?
- Was process sufficient?
- Was reviewer qualified?
- Was source accessible?
- Did technology create an avoidable failure?
- Did organizational incentives contribute?

Focus on process improvement rather than scapegoating.

---

# 76. RESEARCH INTEGRITY

Any evidence of:

- fabricated data,
- fabricated citation,
- fabricated quotation,
- fabricated manuscript,
- deliberately misleading attribution,

must be escalated immediately.

Potential action:

- investigation,
- correction,
- withdrawal,
- retraction,
- governance review.

---

# 77. HISTORICAL UNCERTAINTY

A successful dispute outcome may be:

> **“Unresolved with documented evidence.”**

This is an acceptable and sometimes preferable scholarly result.

---

# 78. EDITORIAL LANGUAGE FOR UNCERTAINTY

Use:

- “diperkirakan”
- “menurut sumber...”
- “dalam tradisi...”
- “belum ditemukan bukti yang cukup...”
- “sumber-sumber berbeda...”
- “statusnya belum dapat dipastikan...”
- “evidence yang tersedia saat ini...”

Avoid false certainty:

- “pasti” when not justified,
- “terbukti” when evidence does not establish it,
- “satu-satunya” without comprehensive support.

---

# 79. PUBLIC CONFIDENCE

Trust is increased when readers can see:

- original publication,
- correction,
- evidence,
- current version,
- reason for change.

The objective is not to appear infallible.

The objective is to be **correctable and auditable**.

- routine correction → editorial authority,

# 89. ANTI-DEFENSIVENESS RULE

# 90. ANTI-OVER-CORRECTION RULE

Do not change an article merely because:

- someone dislikes the wording,
- a political/community pressure appears,
- a sponsor requests favorable treatment,
- a popular commenter disagrees,
- a tradition holder asks for removal without relevant evidence.

Correction requires evidence or a valid rights/privacy basis.

---

# 91. DISPUTE TERMINATION

A dispute may be closed when:

- evidence has been fully assessed,
- correction implemented,
- correction rejected with reason,
- unresolved status documented,
- requester has had reasonable opportunity to provide evidence,
- appeal period/process completed where applicable.

Closing a dispute does not prevent reopening if genuinely new evidence appears.

---

# 92. REOPENING

Reopen when:

- new primary evidence appears,
- new scholarly publication materially changes the evidence base,
- source authenticity changes,
- significant new testimony emerges,
- previous review contained a material process error.

---

# 93. PERIODIC REVIEW

High-risk disputed content should have a review date.

Suggested:

- S0/S1 → normal editorial audit
- S2 → periodic review
- S3 → defined review interval

Review dates depend on historical importance and evidence volatility.
