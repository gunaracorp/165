    32→## 2. THE 165 EDITORIAL CONSTITUTION
    33→
    34→Every important page should follow five rules.
    35→
    36→### Rule A — Provenance before eloquence
    37→Every historical claim should have a source, author/institution, date, and—where relevant—page/manuscript reference.
    38→
    39→### Rule B — Three truth layers
    40→Label information as:
    41→- **Documented History** — supported by traceable historical/academic/archival sources.
    42→- **TQN/Institutional Tradition** — a lineage, recollection, hagiographic account, or institutional narrative presented as such.
    43→- **165 Interpretation** — a contemporary educational or strategic synthesis created by 165.
    44→
    45→### Rule C — No invented silsilah
    46→165 should never manufacture, “complete,” or silently reconcile a lineage that is contested or insufficiently documented. Publish competing versions with attribution and source notes when needed.
    47→
    48→### Rule D — Spiritual authority is not created by a website
    49→Website membership, badges, subscriptions, or digital status must never be presented as equivalent to talqin, bai‘at, khirqah, ijazah, kemursyidan, or other forms of authorized spiritual transmission.
    50→
    51→### Rule E — Preserve the difference between education and initiation
    52→Educational content can be public. Restricted spiritual practices, confidential guidance, or materials that a particular TQN authority considers non-public should only be published with appropriate authorization.

    56→## 3. HISTORY: FROM THE UPSTREAM TO THE DIGITAL PRESENT
    57→
    58→### 3.1 The wider Sufi context
    59→Tarekat should be introduced within the wider history of Islamic spirituality rather than appearing to begin abruptly with one organization. 165 should explain the relationship among **Islam — shari‘ah — tasawwuf — tariqah — adab — dzikr — suluk — ihsan**, while making clear that these terms have varied usages across periods and schools.
    60→
    61→The website should avoid treating one later institutional form as identical to the entire history of Sufism.
    62→
    63→### 3.2 Qadiriyya and Shaykh `Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani
    64→`Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani (1077/78–1166) was a Hanbali scholar, preacher and Sufi shaykh associated with Baghdad; the Qadiri order took his name and developed historically after him. Encyclopaedia Iranica identifies him as the eponymous founder of the Qadiri order.  
    65→
    66→**165 lesson:** authority is preserved through learning, adab, practice and transmission—not merely through branding.
    67→
    68→### 3.3 Naqshbandiyya and Baha al-Din Naqshband
    69→Baha al-Din Naqshband (1318–1389) became the eponym of the Naqshbandiyya, one of the major Sufi orders. Encyclopaedia Iranica notes the importance of disciplined adherence to shari‘ah and silent dhikr within the early Naqshbandi tradition.  
    70→
    71→**165 lesson:** inwardness and disciplined conduct should not be separated from outward ethical life.
    72→
    73→### 3.4 Ahmad Khatib Sambas and the TQN formation
    74→Modern academic literature commonly identifies Shaykh Ahmad Khatib Sambas (1803–1875) as the Nusantara shaykh who formulated the Qadiriyah wa Naqsyabandiyah synthesis in the nineteenth century. Research based on manuscripts of *Fath al-‘Arifin* provides evidence that Ahmad Khatib had transmission links through both Qadiriyah and Naqshbandiyah.  
    75→
    76→**165 lesson:** TQN carries an important Nusantara intellectual history; it should be documented as a serious historical and scholarly subject, not merely as folklore.
    77→
    78→### 3.5 Transmission into Java
    79→Research on the genealogy and spread of TQN in Java identifies major transmission lines through figures such as Shaykh Abdul Karim Banten, Shaykh Ahmad Thalhah Cirebon, and Shaykh Muhammad Hasbullah Madura, followed by several major centers in Java including Suryalaya, Rejoso, Mranggen and Pangentongan.  
    80→
    81→**165 lesson:** the history of TQN is a network, not a single straight line.
    82→
    83→### 3.6 Suryalaya and Abah Sepuh
    84→The official history of Pondok Pesantren Suryalaya places the establishment of the pesantren at Godebag on 5 September 1905 and describes the role of Shaykh Tholhah bin Talabudin of Cirebon in supporting and authorizing Abah Sepuh within the TQN tradition. The exact biographical chronology should always be sourced because some details differ across institutional and scholarly materials.  
    85→
    86→**165 lesson:** institutions survive when spiritual teaching becomes disciplined education, community service and stewardship of place.
    87→
    88→### 3.7 Abah Anom
    89→The Suryalaya record describes KH A. Shohibulwafa Tajul Arifin (Abah Anom) as born in 1915 and succeeding Abah Sepuh in 1956. Under his leadership, Suryalaya expanded education, community development and the Inabah rehabilitation initiative, alongside the continued dissemination of TQN. Academic literature also studies his educational and social roles.  
    90→
    91→**165 lesson:** a spiritual tradition can generate institutions that respond to real social needs while remaining rooted in its ethical vocabulary.
    92→
    93→### 3.8 From physical heritage to digital heritage
    94→This is the point where **165 begins as a contemporary project**. 165 should not claim to be a historical continuation unless that institutional authority is formally established. Its role can be defined more modestly and powerfully: **to document, preserve, explain, connect and make discoverable the history and heritage of TQN for future generations.**
    95→
    96→**165 lesson:** the digital age creates a new amanah—preventing loss, distortion, disappearance and fragmentation of knowledge.

   356→## 12. ARCHIVE & PRESERVATION POLICY
   357→
   358→165 should behave like a digital archive, not only a blog.
   359→
   360→### Minimum preservation stack
   361→- master files kept separately from web derivatives
   362→- 3-2-1 backup strategy
   363→- checksums / fixity checks for archival files
   364→- versioned metadata
   365→- rights records
   366→- controlled vocabularies
   367→- persistent identifiers
   368→- periodic restoration tests
   369→- documented ingest workflow
   370→
   371→For images/manuscripts, store an archival master and separate web derivatives. For important media, retain the original acquisition file where rights allow.
   372→
   373→### Archive ingest workflow
   374→Receive → identify → rights check → scan/transfer → checksum → metadata → description → editorial review → publish derivative → preserve master → audit.

   380→For every serious historical page:
   381→
   382→**Primary sources:** manuscripts, archival documents, institutional records, contemporary testimony.  
   383→**Secondary sources:** scholarly books and peer-reviewed research.  
   384→**Tertiary sources:** encyclopedias, summaries and educational pages.
   385→
   386→When sources disagree:
   387→> “Version A — source …”
   388→> “Version B — source …”
   389→> “165 editorial note — evidence currently available …”
   390→
   391→Do not silently select the most flattering version.

   679→## 19. A MODEL ARTICLE TEMPLATE
   680→
   681→# Title
   682→
   683→**Summary**  
   684→Two or three sentences.
   685→
   686→**What is documented?**  
   687→Short factual section.
   688→
   689→**Sources**  
   690→Primary and secondary references.
   691→
   692→**Historical context**  
   693→Explanation.
   694→
   695→**TQN tradition / institutional account**  
   696→Clearly attributed.
   697→
   698→**What scholars say**  
   699→Attributed where applicable.
   700→
   701→**Hikmah untuk zaman ini**  
   702→165 interpretation.
   703→
   704→**Related people / places / texts**  
   705→Linked records.
   706→
   707→**Archive metadata**  
   708→ID, version, rights, editorial review date.

   712→## 20. THE 165 QUALITY BAR
   713→
   714→Every flagship page should pass six tests:
   715→
   716→**Truth** — Are claims sourced?  
   717→**Context** — Is historical context adequate?  
   718→**Transmission** — Is lineage/authority distinguished from biography?  
   719→**Language** — Is translation careful?  
   720→**Preservation** — Can the record be found and understood years later?  
   721→**Usefulness** — Does the page help a reader do or understand something?
